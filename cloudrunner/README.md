# Cloudrunner

A thermal-gliding game over low-poly valleys. Your sailplane has no engine: it only ever comes down, unless you find rising air. Find a white column of rising air under a cloud, circle inside it to climb, then spend that height gliding to the next striped beacon. The air keeps changing while you fly: thermals build, drift with the wind and collapse, so the cloud you are heading for may be gone when you get there and you have to pick another.

- **Daily task:** four beacons, the same for every pilot each day (UTC). The flight ends when you reach the last one, touch the ground, or after six minutes. The day's leaderboard ranks the most beacons first, then the fastest time, and your friends hear about it when you take the lead.
- **Free flight:** a fresh valley every time and beacons without end. It is over when you land out; the board keeps your most beacons in one flight.
- **How to fly:** follow the cyan "Lift" chip under the top bar into a white column under a cloud. When the climb meter at the bottom turns green and reads RISING, the screen edges glow cyan and it beeps: hold a turn (about half stick, or an arrow key) to circle and climb. Then follow the red "Beacon" chip to the striped beacon and cross its red ring. Short tips show the first time you find lift and the first time you lose it.
- **Controls:** a thumb stick banks and pitches the glider. Push the nose down to trade height for speed, ease it up to slow down; let go and the glider holds about 108 km/h. On a keyboard, arrow keys or WASD steer (a half-bank turn; hold Shift for a steep one) and Space launches. A button flips the stick's up and down, and another turns the sound off.
- **One colour per meaning:** cyan and white is rising air, red is a beacon, green on the climb meter is climbing and red is sinking. The map uses the same colours.
- **Seeing the air:** each thermal is a cool cyan column from the ground up into its cloud, with upward chevrons and bright streaks rushing up it, densest in the core; close by, a cyan ring on the ground marks where its lift is. A stronger thermal draws a denser, brighter column. The lift is strongest in the middle and eases off toward the edge, so the climb meter rises steadily as you close on the core, and air drawn in at the column's foot slowly pulls a circling glider toward the centre. A thermal builds over a few seconds, lasts a minute or so, then fades: its column and its cloud fade together. Lift stops at the cloud base, about 850 m up. Between thermals the air sinks gently, more in some places than others, shown only by faint grey specks drifting down; the wind gives slope lift low over the hillsides it blows up, which have a pale cyan tint. Every flight starts with one strong thermal straight ahead.
- **The climb meter:** the bar and number show your climb rate in metres per second. Climbing, the whole meter turns green and reads RISING in large type; sinking, it reads SINKING in red. It beeps while you climb: higher and faster the stronger the lift.
- **Beacons:** a beacon is a red-and-white striped mast in a red ring on the ground. Fly over the ring at any height to take it. A numbered red board on top faces you, with the distance; the next beacon's mast always reaches up to your height, so its board is at eye level, while later beacons are short, thin and faint. Two chips under the top bar point the way from your nose: a red "Beacon 1 · 750 m" and a cyan "Lift · 320 m" to the nearest live thermal (hidden while you are in lift). The map shows the thermals around you in cyan, the next beacons in red and the route.
- **The glide:** about 9 metres forward for every metre down at 108 km/h in still air, and much worse when you dive. From the launch height you cannot reach the last beacon without climbing.
- **Ideal path:** turn on "Ideal path" beside the stick to see what an autopilot would do right now: a dashed line to the thermal it would use, up it, and on to the next beacon.
- **Your progress is saved:** daily bests and your free-flight record stay with you, and a flight cut short by closing the app still counts.
