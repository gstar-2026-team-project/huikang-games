# Cloudrunner

A thermal-gliding game over low-poly valleys. Your sailplane has no engine: it only ever comes down, unless you find rising air. Fly to a cloud, circle in the golden column of rising air under it to climb, then spend that height gliding to the next beacon. The air keeps changing while you fly: thermals build, drift with the wind and collapse, so the cloud you are heading for may be gone when you get there and you have to pick another.

- **Daily task:** four turnpoints, each marked by a beacon, the same for every pilot each day (UTC). The flight ends when you reach the last one, touch the ground, or after six minutes. The day's leaderboard ranks the most turnpoints first, then the fastest time, and your friends hear about it when you take the lead.
- **Free flight:** a fresh valley every time and turnpoints without end. It is over when you land out; the board keeps your most turnpoints in one flight.
- **How to fly:** fly to a cloud. When the variometer at the bottom of the screen turns green and starts to beep, hold a turn (about half stick) to circle and climb. When it turns red again, glide on to the numbered beacon and cross its ring. Short tips show the first time you find lift and the first time you lose it.
- **Controls:** a thumb stick banks and pitches the glider. Push the nose down to trade height for speed, ease it up to slow down; let go and the glider holds about 108 km/h. On a keyboard, arrow keys or WASD steer and Space launches. A button flips the stick's up and down, and another turns the sound off.
- **Seeing the air:** each thermal is a soft golden column from the ground up to its cloud, with specks streaming up it, densest in the core. The lift is strongest in the middle and eases off toward the edge, so the variometer rises steadily as you close on the core, and air drawn in at the column's foot slowly pulls a circling glider toward the centre. A thermal builds over a few seconds, lasts a minute or so, then fades: its column thins and its cloud dissolves. Lift stops at the cloud base, about 850 m up. Between thermals the air sinks gently, more in some places than others, and the wind gives slope lift low over the hillsides it blows up, which glow warm. Every flight starts with one strong thermal straight ahead.
- **The variometer:** the bar and number show your climb rate in metres per second, green up and red down, and it beeps while you climb: higher and faster the stronger the lift.
- **Beacons:** a beacon is a tall beam of light with a ring on the ground. Fly over the ring at any height to take it. A numbered marker on the beam shows which beacon it is and how far away; the next one is bright and later ones dim. An arrow at the edge of the screen points to the next beacon when it is out of view, with its distance, and the map shows the thermals around you, the next beacons and the route.
- **The glide:** about 9 metres forward for every metre down at 108 km/h in still air, and much worse when you dive. From the launch height you cannot reach the last beacon without climbing.
- **Ideal path:** turn on "Ideal path" beside the stick to see what an autopilot would do right now: a dashed line to the thermal it would use, up it, and on to the next beacon.
- **Your progress is saved:** daily bests and your free-flight record stay with you, and a flight cut short by closing the app still counts.
